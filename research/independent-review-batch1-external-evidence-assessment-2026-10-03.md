## IV-001

**Claim:** “Stars and galaxies change/evolve over time.”

**Independent evidence reviewed:**
- NASA Science, “Stars”: NASA states that stars have life cycles, their properties change as they age, and describes birth, life and death stages.
- NASA Science, “Types of Stars”: NASA describes stars changing between stages such as main sequence and red giant, with evolutionary processes dependent on stellar mass.
- NASA Science, “Evolution” (Galaxies): NASA describes galaxy evolution and observations of galaxies at different evolutionary stages.

**Assessment:** The cited external evidence directly supports the broad descriptive claim that stars and galaxies evolve/change over time.

**Limitation / counter-evidence:** The claim is broad. A final verification record should define what “change/evolve” means operationally and specify the exact observation/test being accepted.

**Status:** EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED; independent promotion still pending.

## IV-002

**Claim:** “Galaxies can collide and merge and their structures evolve.”

**Independent evidence reviewed:**
- NASA Science, “Evolution” (Galaxies): NASA documents galaxy collisions, gravitational deformation, mergers and resulting structural changes.
- NASA Science, “What Happens When Galaxies Collide?”: NASA describes collisions producing new forms and shapes.
- NASA Science, “Galaxies Over Time”: NASA describes ongoing galaxy formation/evolution and observations of colliding and merging galaxies.

**Assessment:** The independent evidence directly supports the existence of galaxy collisions, mergers and structural evolution.

**Limitation / counter-evidence:** A final verification should distinguish “galaxies can collide/merge” from stronger claims about how frequently this happens or whether every merger produces a particular morphology.

**Status:** EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED; independent promotion still pending.

## IV-003

**Claim:** “Human activity affects climate, ecosystems and biodiversity.”

**Independent evidence reviewed:**
- IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report: assesses human-induced climate change and recognizes interdependence among climate, ecosystems, biodiversity and human societies.
- IPCC AR6 Summary for Policymakers: reports observed widespread impacts of climate change on terrestrial, freshwater and ocean ecosystems and biodiversity, and assesses observed physical changes increasingly attributed to human influence.
- IPCC WGII material: assesses climate-change impacts across species, ecosystems and ecosystem services and discusses human interventions affecting ecosystem resilience.

**Assessment:** The external evidence supports the claim at its stated broad level.

**Limitation / counter-evidence:** “Affects” is non-directional and very broad. A final verification should specify which human activities, which climate/ecological variables and what causal attribution threshold are being tested.

**Status:** EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED; independent promotion still pending.

## IV-004

**Claim:** “First-person self-knowledge and prereflective self-consciousness are established objects of philosophical analysis.”

**Independent evidence reviewed:**
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Self-Knowledge”: surveys self-knowledge as a substantial area of philosophical analysis, including first-person authority and methods of self-knowledge.
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Phenomenological Approaches to Self-Consciousness”: describes prereflective self-consciousness as a central phenomenological concept and distinguishes it from reflective self-consciousness.

**Assessment:** The claim is supported as a claim about the existence of established philosophical analysis/discussion. This does not establish any stronger empirical or metaphysical claim about the nature of consciousness.

**Limitation / counter-evidence:** Philosophical schools disagree about the interpretation and theoretical status of self-consciousness; the review should therefore preserve the narrower claim actually made.

**Status:** EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED; independent promotion still pending.
