## IV-001 — Stars and galaxies change/evolve over time

Claim:
> Stars and galaxies change/evolve over time.

Independent evidence:
- NASA states that stars have life cycles and that their properties change as they age.
- NASA's galaxy-evolution material documents ongoing galaxy collisions, mergers and structural changes.

Sources:
- https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/
- https://science.nasa.gov/universe/galaxies/evolution/
- https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/galaxies-over-time/

Assessment:
Evidence supports the ordinary astronomical proposition that stars and galaxies undergo temporal evolution.

Counter-evidence / limitation:
This does not establish any broader metaphysical proposition that may be associated with cosmic evolution.

Status: PENDING_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW

## IV-002 — Galaxies can collide and merge and their structures evolve

Claim:
> Galaxies can collide and merge and their structures evolve.

Independent evidence:
- NASA describes galaxy collisions, deformation through gravitational interaction, and mergers that can produce a single larger galaxy with a different structure.
- NASA also documents continuing galaxy assembly through mergers.

Sources:
- https://science.nasa.gov/universe/galaxies/evolution/
- https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/science-highlights/galaxy-details-and-mergers/
- https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/galaxies-over-time/

Assessment:
The stated astronomical claim is externally source-checkable.

Counter-evidence / limitation:
Individual galaxy evolution histories vary; the sources do not support extrapolating this claim into unrelated universal mechanisms.

Status: PENDING_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW

## IV-003 — Human activity affects climate, ecosystems and biodiversity

Claim:
> Human activity affects climate, ecosystems and biodiversity.

Independent evidence:
- IPCC AR6 WGII assesses interactions among climate, ecosystems (including biodiversity) and human society.
- The IPCC states that human society causes climate change and impacts ecosystems, while also documenting impacts from human activities on biodiversity hotspots.
- The assessment distinguishes climate-change effects from other anthropogenic pressures such as habitat loss, pollution and over-exploitation.

Sources:
- https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/
- https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/summary-for-policymakers/
- https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/ccp1/

Assessment:
The claim is externally source-checkable and supported at the level stated.

Counter-evidence / limitation:
Attribution varies by phenomenon. The IPCC distinguishes climate-related effects from other human pressures and notes that not every observed ecological change can be attributed to climate change alone.

Status: PENDING_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW

## IV-004 — First-person self-knowledge and prereflective self-consciousness are established objects of philosophical analysis

Claim:
> First-person self-knowledge and prereflective self-consciousness are established objects of philosophical analysis.

Independent evidence:
- The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy treats self-knowledge as knowledge of one's own mental states and reviews competing accounts of its epistemic status and acquisition.
- Its phenomenology entry discusses prereflective self-consciousness as a topic concerning the first-personal givenness of experience.

Sources:
- https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/self-knowledge/
- https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/self-consciousness-phenomenological/

Assessment:
The claim is supported as a descriptive claim about philosophical scholarship.

Counter-evidence / limitation:
The existence of philosophical analysis does not establish that any particular theory of self-consciousness is objectively true.

Status: PENDING_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW
