# SHIRMANI — First Client Pilot Demonstration

**Status:** Illustrative demonstration only — not a client commission, paid order, or independently researched report.  
**Service examples:** Hindi/English editing, website copy, and structured research-summary writing.  
**Purpose:** Let a prospective customer inspect the format and quality before agreeing to a real scope.

---

## Example A — Editing and clarity

### Supplied draft (fictional)
> Our learning centre is providing many type of class for student and we are trying to make education better. Student can come and take help for study and other activity.

### Edited version
> Our learning centre offers a range of classes and learning activities for students. Learners can receive support with their studies, build practical skills, and take part in guided activities in a welcoming environment.

### Editorial notes
- Corrected grammar and subject–verb agreement.
- Replaced vague wording with clearer, reader-friendly language.
- Preserved the original meaning without inventing services, results, or statistics.

## Example B — Website / blog copy

### Headline
**A welcoming place to learn, practise and grow**

### Introduction
Every learner benefits from clear guidance, a supportive environment, and opportunities to practise. Our learning-centre concept brings these elements together through structured sessions and activities designed around learners' needs.

### What visitors can expect
- **Guided learning:** Sessions organised around an agreed learning goal.
- **Practical activities:** Opportunities to practise skills, ask questions, and build confidence.
- **Clear communication:** Information about schedules, eligibility, fees, and materials shared before enrolment.

### Call to action
**Have a question or want to understand whether a programme is suitable?** Contact the centre to discuss your learning goals, available sessions, timings, and fees before making a decision.

### Publication note
This is sample copy for a fictional centre. Replace all generic statements with the organisation's verified details before publication.

## Example C — Source-aware research-summary format

**Illustrative topic:** Planning an accessible community learning programme.

| Section | What the commissioned deliverable contains |
|---|---|
| Question | The specific decision the customer needs to make |
| Scope | Location, audience, time period, and exclusions |
| Sources | 3–5 credible, relevant sources where available and agreed |
| Findings | Concise claims linked to source URLs |
| Limitations | Missing evidence, uncertainty, and conflicting findings |
| Practical options | Feasible next steps, with assumptions stated |
| References | Source title, publisher, date, and URL |

No findings or citations are fabricated in this demonstration. A real research summary is produced only after the customer agrees the question and source requirements.

## Quality and handoff checklist

- [ ] Customer brief and intended audience confirmed
- [ ] Scope, word count, language, deadline, and revision limit agreed in writing
- [ ] All factual claims checked against suitable sources
- [ ] Client-supplied material and usage permissions checked
- [ ] Names, figures, spelling, accessibility, and formatting reviewed
- [ ] Final price and payment schedule agreed before work starts
- [ ] Final files delivered through the agreed channel
- [ ] Delivery and customer acceptance recorded

## How to commission a real pilot

1. Share the topic, objective, audience, language, and desired format.
2. Receive a written scope, final price, deadline, and revision limit.
3. Approve the proposal and payment terms in writing.
4. Work begins after the agreed start condition is met.
5. Review the delivered files against the agreed scope.
