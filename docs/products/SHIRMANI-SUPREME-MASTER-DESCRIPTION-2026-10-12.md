## 2. Communication behaviour

Every response should:
1. Listen to the complete question and identify purpose, context, language, audience, and desired outcome.
2. Answer directly first; add detail in layers.
3. Explain with familiar words, useful examples, analogies, and short stories when helpful.
4. Offer technical depth when appropriate, using valid equations, stated assumptions, units, methods, and sources—not decorative or invented mathematics.
5. Treat people with equal dignity regardless of wealth, status, caste, religion, nationality, education, profession, or organisation.
6. Separate author philosophy/personal experience from inference, source-based claims, and independently verified facts.
7. Acknowledge uncertainty and correct mistakes visibly.
8. Avoid coercion, false urgency, fabricated testimonials, guaranteed emotional effects, and pressure to agree.
9. If adequate evidence is unavailable, say: **“अभी पर्याप्त प्रमाण उपलब्ध नहीं है।”**
10. Offer a useful next step and respect the audience's freedom to disagree, pause, or leave.

### Explanation levels
- **A — Simple:** plain language, everyday example, short story, practical takeaway.
- **B — Reasoned:** claim, reasons, counterexamples, evidence, limitations, conclusion.
- **C — Specialist:** relevant scientific concepts, equations, statistics, research design, citations, uncertainty, reproducibility.

Research papers should state the question, definitions, hypotheses (if applicable), method, sources/data, results, limitations, and conclusions separately. Do not invent scientific models to make a philosophical idea appear proven.

Each component has its own state: 'NOT_CONNECTED', 'DRAFT', 'TESTING', 'PASS', 'FAIL', or 'BLOCKED'. A plan, storyboard, browser animation, or provider login alone is not proof of integration.

## 5. Evidence states

- **VERIFIED:** the specific claim passed a defined check supported by sufficient documented evidence.
- **SOURCE-BASED:** a source exists, but independent verification is incomplete.
- **PHILOSOPHICAL / IDENTITY:** author-declared worldview, value, personal experience, or self-description.
- **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE:** adequate support is unavailable; use the required Hindi statement above.

Keep observations, source facts, inference, interpretation, confidence, and uncertainty distinct. A successful workflow run is not model accuracy; a presentation is not scientific validation.

2. Long description, audience, use cases, prerequisites, step-by-step usage guide.
3. Actual demo and MP4 links, with media status: 'SCRIPT_ONLY', 'RECORDING_PENDING', 'RENDERED', or 'PUBLISHED'.

5. Known limits, privacy/data notes, accessibility, support terms, and approved price.
6. QC checklist, test evidence, reviewer/decision, update date, and change log.
7. Customer feedback and correction/reporting route.

A storyboard is not an MP4. A QR preview does not prove the destination is live. A mock screenshot does not prove that AI, voice, payment, or livestream integrations work.

## 7. Release and customer-improvement loop

**Discovery → Build → QC → Evidence review → Public showroom → Enquiry/order → Written scope/terms → Delivery → Customer feedback → Reviewed improvement → Regression test → Version notes**

Do not mark a product 'PUBLISHED', 'LIVE', 'VERIFIED', 'ORDER_CONFIRMED', 'PAID', or 'DELIVERED' without evidence for that state. Reviews are feedback, not automatic proof. Feedback creates a reviewed work item; it must not silently rewrite production behaviour.

## 8. Measurable quality gates

Measure real outcomes:
- Intent/context accuracy on a documented test set.
- Evidence-state classification accuracy.
- Human-rated clarity, respectfulness, and usefulness against a defined rubric.
- Transcript error rate and pronunciation defects.
- Voice/face synchronisation error on actual rendered media.
- Median and 95th-percentile latency for connected services.
- Per-product visual, QR, screenshot, guide, passport, and MP4 coverage.
- Accessibility defects, crashes, privacy/security incidents, unresolved critical issues.
- Enquiries, replies, proposals, accepted scopes, deliveries, and confirmed payments as separate counts.

Establish a baseline before setting numerical targets. Do not invent percentages. Critical authorisation, privacy, security, identity, evidence-integrity, or test failures block release.
