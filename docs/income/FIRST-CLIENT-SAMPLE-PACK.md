## 1. Website/blog sample — English

### A practical checklist for making a small-business website easier to trust

A useful business website should answer five questions quickly: What do you offer? Who is it for? What does it cost or how can someone request a quote? How can a visitor contact you? What happens next?

Start with a clear headline that describes the service in ordinary language. Put one primary action near the top, such as “Request a quote” or “View services.” Avoid displaying several competing calls to action with equal emphasis.

Make contact details easy to find. Explain the steps after an enquiry, including what information the customer should provide and when they can expect a response. If a price is only an estimate, label it as indicative and explain when the final quote will be confirmed.

Show evidence responsibly. Use real samples with permission, explain what each sample demonstrates, and never invent customer logos, reviews, certifications, results, or sales figures. If a feature is still a prototype, label it as a prototype. If a payment or booking system is not connected, do not imply that a transaction can be completed online.

Finally, test the page on a phone as well as a desktop. Check that text is readable, links work, forms give clear feedback, and a visitor can return to the main navigation. A smaller page that is clear and reliable is more useful than a crowded page full of unverified promises.

अंत में वेबसाइट को मोबाइल और कंप्यूटर—दोनों पर जाँचें। अक्षर पढ़ने योग्य हों, लिंक खुलें, फॉर्म स्पष्ट संदेश दे और मुख्य मेन्यू तक लौटना आसान हो। साफ और भरोसेमंद छोटा पेज, ऐसे बड़े पेज से अधिक उपयोगी है जिसमें बहुत-से अप्रमाणित दावे हों।

## 3. Research-summary demonstration — digital inclusion

**Question:** What should a small organisation check before publishing a digital-literacy guide?

### Summary
A useful guide should be accessible, understandable, safe to use, and easy to verify. The organisation should define its intended audience, test the guide with representative readers, explain unfamiliar terms, and provide practical steps rather than assuming every reader has the same device, connection, language fluency, or prior experience.

A responsible guide should also cover privacy and security basics: protect passwords, be cautious with unexpected links, check the source of claims, and avoid sharing sensitive information unnecessarily. Instructions should distinguish verified facts from recommendations and should be reviewed when tools or interfaces change.

### Source notes
- UNESCO — Digital literacy resources and publications: https://www.unesco.org/en/digital-competencies-skills
- International Telecommunication Union (ITU) — Digital inclusion: https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-D/Digital-Inclusion/Pages/default.aspx
- Government of India, National Digital Literacy Mission information portal: https://www.pmgdisha.in/
