# Humanity–Earth Service Model

## Mission framing
The Yatharth platform can encode the author's stated objective of improving human–nature relationships, protecting Earth and expanding access to useful services. These are mission objectives, not established scientific conclusions.

## Service pillars
### Basic needs
Explore lawful, sustainable models for access to food, clothing, shelter, healthcare and education. Do not promise universal free provision without a verified funding, delivery and eligibility mechanism.

### Human development
Learning, skills, employment, freelancing, entrepreneurship, creativity and community participation.

### Earth stewardship
Environmental education, conservation projects, biodiversity information, pollution reporting, restoration participation and measurable impact dashboards.

### Well-being and dignity
Accessible information, social connection, complaint channels, anti-abuse controls and support referrals.

### Justice and fairness
Transparent rules, evidence-based case handling, conflict-of-interest controls, appeals and independent human review for consequential decisions.

## Measurement
Track service reach, accessibility, affordability, satisfaction, resolution times, environmental impact indicators and independently reviewed outcomes.

## Governance boundary
The platform should support democratic and lawful institutions rather than replacing constitutional government, courts or elected public authorities. AI can assist administration and analysis; it should not become an unaccountable supreme authority.

## Implementation principle
Family -> community -> city -> region -> country -> global ecosystem can be represented as nested service scopes, while preserving individual rights, local context, lawful governance and appeal mechanisms.
