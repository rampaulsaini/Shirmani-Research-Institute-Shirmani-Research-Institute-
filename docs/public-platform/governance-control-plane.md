# Yatharth Public Platform — Human-Centered Governance Control Plane

## Purpose

This module translates the user's stated goal of a family-to-global public system into a **neutral, rights-preserving technical architecture**. It is a design specification, not a claim that any government, court, president, parliament, or public-service system has been replaced.

The architecture is built around one rule:

> **AI/ML/NLP/Automission may assist, monitor, explain, test and route; it must not become an unreviewable sovereign authority.**

## Constitutional layers

1. **People / constitutional rights** — source of public legitimacy and protected rights.
2. **Legislative function** — creates and changes public rules through lawful procedures.
3. **Executive / administration** — implements enacted rules and public services.
4. **Independent judicial function** — reviews disputes, legality and rights.
5. **Independent audit and ombuds function** — investigates conflicts, failures and complaints.
6. **AI/ML/NLP/Automission control plane** — evidence, monitoring, simulation, routing and bounded automation.
7. **Public transparency layer** — publishes decisions, policies, service metrics and audit trails subject to privacy/security limits.

No layer receives unlimited authority from an AI model.

## Supreme-control boundary

The requested concept of a “supreme control” is implemented here as a **supreme constitutional constraint**, not a single person or AI agent.

Hard constraints:
- no AI-only criminal, welfare, medical, educational or employment decision;
- no irreversible high-impact action without an authorized human/legal process;
- every automated decision has an owner, reason, policy version and audit record;
- affected people receive notice and an appeal/review route where applicable;
- emergency powers expire automatically unless renewed under lawful procedure;
- system operators cannot silently rewrite historical audit records;
- research claims cannot be promoted to VERIFIED by workflow completion alone.

## AI agent hierarchy

### L1 — Intake
Receives requests, reports and evidence.

### L2 — Classification
Normalizes the request and assigns a policy domain.

### L3 — Evidence
Collects provenance, counter-evidence and uncertainty.

### L4 — Analysis
Runs ML/NLP classification, summarization, contradiction checks and simulations.

### L5 — Policy simulation
Shows expected trade-offs and affected groups. It does not choose the policy.

### L6 — Human authorization
A designated lawful authority reviews the evidence and makes the decision.

### L7 — Independent review
Appeal, judicial or audit review can challenge the decision.

### L8 — Public transparency
Publishes non-sensitive decision metadata, policy version, audit status and service outcomes.

## Nature, Earth, humanity and all living beings

The platform can maintain measurable protection objectives such as:
- biodiversity and habitat indicators;
- air, water and soil indicators;
- climate and disaster-risk indicators;
- food, shelter, healthcare and education access;
- accessibility and inclusion;
- animal and plant protection;
- resource-use and restoration indicators.

These become **measurable policy objectives**, not metaphysical claims.

## Universal basic services

The architecture may represent public-service commitments for:
- food security;
- clothing/basic goods;
- safe housing;
- essential healthcare;
- education;
- clean water and sanitation;
- basic digital access;
- emergency assistance.

Whether a service is legally guaranteed, publicly funded, free at point of use, subsidized, or otherwise provided must remain a jurisdiction-specific policy/legal decision. The platform must display the actual status rather than label an aspirational service as already available.

## Rights and appeals

Every high-impact workflow should expose:
- decision status;
- responsible authority;
- policy/rule version;
- evidence references;
- uncertainty/confidence where meaningful;
- reason code;
- appeal/review route;
- deadline;
- accessibility/language support.

## Audit and anti-capture controls

Required controls:
- append-only audit events;
- dual authorization for high-impact policy changes;
- separation of deployer and reviewer roles;
- immutable policy/version identifiers;
- conflict-of-interest declaration;
- public change log;
- emergency-action expiry;
- periodic independent audit;
- model/version rollback;
- incident response and redress.

## Verification boundary

The following are engineering evidence, not independent verification:
- CI success;
- generated AI reports;
- workflow completion;
- database records;
- hashes;
- publication;
- API health;
- author testimony.

Independent verification requires qualifying independent human review and an explicit evidence contract.

## Design status

**ARCHITECTURE / RESEARCH DESIGN.**

It is not a deployed government, court, welfare system, universal basic-service provider, global authority, or legal currency.
