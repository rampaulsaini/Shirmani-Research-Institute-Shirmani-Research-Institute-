# Public Welfare & Satisfaction Dashboard Contract

## Objective
Public success is measured by user value and public-interest outcomes, not only engagement or time spent.

## Dashboard dimensions

### Human wellbeing
- food access
- basic shelter access
- healthcare access
- education access
- clean water
- sanitation
- accessibility
- complaint resolution

### Nature and planetary stewardship
- forest protection/restoration indicators
- water/ocean indicators
- soil indicators
- biodiversity indicators
- air-quality indicators
- climate indicators
- space-environment research indicators

### Digital platform quality
- service availability
- response time
- safety incidents
- privacy incidents
- successful support resolution
- creator outcomes
- learning outcomes
- marketplace outcomes
- accessibility
- user satisfaction

## Satisfaction measurement
Use multiple measures rather than a single engagement metric:
- task completion;
- reported satisfaction;
- repeat voluntary use;
- complaint resolution;
- creator/worker outcomes;
- learning outcomes;
- safety and trust;
- accessibility;
- public-interest outcomes.

## Universal-service principle
Food, clothing/basic shelter, healthcare and education can be represented as public-policy objectives and research indicators. A dashboard must distinguish:
1. proposed objective;
2. available service;
3. verified coverage;
4. outcome evidence;
5. funding/capacity constraints.

It must never imply universal provision where evidence does not establish it.

## Governance
Public dashboards should disclose methodology, data sources, update time, uncertainty and limitations. High-impact governance decisions remain subject to applicable law, accountable institutions and appeal.
