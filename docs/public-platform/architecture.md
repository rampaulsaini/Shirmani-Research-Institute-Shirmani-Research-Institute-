# Public Platform Architecture

## Layer 1 — Experience

A single responsive interface exposes the major modules without forcing users to understand the internal automation system.

## Layer 2 — Identity and capabilities

Account, profile, preferences, roles, capability grants, reputation signals, consent and privacy settings.

## Layer 3 — Content and transactions

Posts, media, courses, research records, listings, services, orders, subscriptions, support and other domain objects.

## Layer 4 — AI/ML/NLP control plane

Agents classify content, extract entities and claims, route tasks, assist discovery, detect anomalies, generate drafts, run routine QA, and prepare evidence packets.

AI output remains attributable to its model/process and is not automatically equivalent to independent human verification.

## Layer 5 — Automission

- L1 Intake
- L2 Claim/Content classification
- L3 Evidence and retrieval
- L4 Comparison
- L5 Audit/QC
- L6 Verification gate
- L7 Federation
- L8 Public presentation

Each layer emits structured events and failure signals.

## Layer 6 — Trust and governance

Moderation, abuse prevention, privacy controls, appeals, financial safeguards, dispute resolution, independent review and audit logs.

## Layer 7 — Infrastructure

APIs, database/storage, queues, search/indexing, observability, secrets management, backups, CI/CD and disaster recovery.

## High-impact decision rule

Automated recommendations may be used for triage. Account termination, material financial actions, serious disputes, justice outcomes and independent verification status require an appropriate human review/appeal path.

## Non-goals

This architecture does not by itself prove global scale, production readiness, legal currency status, scientific verification, or guaranteed user adoption.
