## Enquiry-to-delivery workflow
1. Prospect visits the public enquiry page and provides topic, audience, language, word limit, deadline, sources, budget, and contact details.
2. Human review confirms feasibility, scope, source quality, and conflicts.
3. Send a written proposal stating deliverables, exclusions, price, delivery date, revision count, payment milestones, and refund/cancellation terms.
4. Begin only after written acceptance and agreed payment arrangement.
5. Deliver draft with sources and uncertainty clearly marked.
6. Complete agreed revisions; request feedback/testimonial only with permission.
7. Record the result in an access-controlled ledger. Never publish client details without consent.

## 30-day plan and measurable targets
- **Days 1–3:** Test enquiry page on mobile and desktop; verify validation, copy/export, and clear wording that the page does not submit orders/payments automatically.
- **Days 4–7:** Prepare three original samples: one research brief, one Hindi article, one English article. Include source notes and publish only material the institute owns or has permission to use.
- **Days 8–14:** Research 20 relevant prospects; send up to five personalised messages per working day. Log actual sends and respect opt-outs.
- **Days 15–21:** Follow up once where appropriate; qualify replies and send proposals only after scope is clear.
- **Days 22–30:** Deliver accepted work, request feedback, and review results.

Track: prospects researched, messages actually sent, replies, qualified enquiries, proposals, accepted orders, cash received, hours spent, direct costs, on-time delivery, revision rounds, and client satisfaction. Use zero when the observed count is zero. Never report proposals as sales or expected revenue as received income.

### Pilot targets (not guarantees)
- 20 verified prospects researched.
- 10–20 personalised messages actually sent and logged.
- 3 qualified replies/enquiries.
- 2 written proposals.
- 1 paid pilot order, if real demand supports it.
- 100% of accepted work has written scope, price, deadline, payment terms, and copyright/source review.

If replies are low, revise the audience or offer before increasing volume. If no paid order occurs, report that honestly and run another test.

## Copyright, product price, and licence clarity
- A product/tool price must say what the customer receives: a deliverable, one-time service, subscription, support, or licence.
- Do not call a product price a licence fee unless the contract actually grants a licence.
- Retain rights in original institute-created work where legally applicable, but third-party libraries, fonts, datasets, media, model APIs, and open-source dependencies remain subject to their own terms.
- Do not promise exclusive ownership of every component forever without an asset/dependency audit and legal review.
- Before selling digital products, define permitted use, redistribution, source files, updates, support, refunds, and licence restrictions in writing.

## Production and automation boundaries
Treat the existing pages as brief-generation tools unless tested otherwise. Before describing a flow as production-ready, verify: deployed HTTPS endpoint and commit; form/accessibility/mobile tests; data protection and deletion controls if data is stored; payment integration and signed webhook verification; email integration and delivery/error logs; opt-out handling and rate limits; security checks, monitoring, incident response, rollback, and end-to-end tests.

Never claim an email was sent, payment received, sale completed, or feature is live without logs or other evidence.
