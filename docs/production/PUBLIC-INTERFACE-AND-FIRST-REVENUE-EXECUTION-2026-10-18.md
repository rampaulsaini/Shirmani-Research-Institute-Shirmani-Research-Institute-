# Public Interface, Production Continuity & First-Revenue Execution Plan
**Date:** 2026-10-18  
**Status:** Execution plan; not evidence of deployed changes, sales, income, or completed external integrations.

## 1. Operating rule: preserve before pruning
The repository contains many concrete browser-local tools and overlapping entry points. Similar names do not prove that a page is useless. Do not delete, rename, or hide any asset merely because it looks duplicated. First map inbound links, unique features, data dependencies, workflows, and public use; then propose a reversible redirect/archive or consolidation and verify references before removal.

## 2. One clear public journey
Make the public homepage answer these questions in this order, with plain language and a visible status on every card:

1. **What is Shirmani Research Institute?** A concise purpose statement, without unverifiable superlatives.
2. **What can I use or buy today?** Start with the currently declared service: Hindi/English writing and research.
3. **Try a real demo.** Give each listed tool a direct demo link and say whether it is a browser-local prototype or a connected service.
4. **Choose a category.** Writing & Research; AI/learning tools; Creator & presentation tools; Product factory; Research/archive; Nature & education; Operations & security.
5. **Request work or a quote.** One consistent enquiry route with objective, topic, language, deliverable/word limit, source links, deadline, budget, contact details, and special instructions.
6. **Understand fulfilment.** Scope → written quote and terms → agreed payment → production → quality review → delivery → feedback/upgrade.
7. **See proof and limitations.** Link to source records, release notes, tests, privacy/terms, and current service status. Do not label a planned capability as live.

Use a simple vertical flow, strong headings, readable mobile layout, consistent card spacing, restrained colors, and one primary action per section. Keep advanced research and internal production tools available in a clearly named “Research & Production Workspace”; do not force the public visitor to understand internal factory terminology.

For each catalogue item, show: name, who it helps, problem solved, actual demo, version, language/accessibility, price or “quote required”, readiness status, data/privacy note, QC state, and next step. Do not show a product as purchasable until scope, delivery, support, and price are defined.

## 4. Truthful capability states
Use one shared vocabulary throughout the site:

- **Working local demo:** runs in the visitor's browser; local-only data unless explicitly stated.
- **Prototype / draft:** useful for exploration; human review required.
- **Integration pending:** provider/API connection not yet proven.
- **Tested:** include test date, exact environment, steps, result, and evidence link.
- **Available for paid delivery:** scope, price, turnaround, and fulfilment terms are explicitly defined.
- **Not verified:** do not imply the capability works.

For HeyGen and the presentation chain, record separate evidence for: authorized asset/source; generated video URL and playback; voice identity/authorization; audio output; lip-sync observation; a set of scripted Q&A prompts and actual responses; latency/failure behavior; and end-to-end test record. A saved video link alone does not prove live Q&A, voice integration, or lip-sync correctness. Never request the user's photo/video again when the supplied repository reference is sufficient; do not claim an external test unless actually executed.

## 5. First-revenue focus: one service, one path
Current declared offer: Hindi/English writing and research. Prioritize a small, deliverable service rather than waiting for the entire platform to be finished.

**Pilot offers (proposed, not final prices):**
- Research summary: question, source links, 1,000–1,500 words, evidence/limitations table, citations, one revision.
- Website/blog content: one page or one article, agreed audience/keywords, draft and one revision.
- Hindi ↔ English editing/adaptation: agreed source length, tone, terminology, and revision limit.

For every enquiry, confirm the customer's objective, deliverables, sources, deadline, price, revision count, ownership/confidentiality expectations, and payment milestones in writing before starting. If a client budget is missing, do not invent a binding quote. Keep samples clearly labelled as illustrative, not client testimonials.

## 6. Seven-day measurable work cycle
Day 1: choose one canonical service page and test the enquiry flow on mobile and desktop.  
Day 2: polish two relevant sample deliverables and label them as samples.  
Day 3: create a short prospect list from verified official organisation pages; record the official source URL and fit rationale.  
Day 4: prepare individually tailored, truthful outreach drafts; review manually before sending.  
Day 5: send only approved messages through an actually available channel; log date and outcome.  
Day 6: follow up once where appropriate and improve the offer using real replies.  
Day 7: report funnel numbers and blockers; do not infer revenue from views, drafts, or sent messages.

Track weekly: qualified prospects identified; official-source URLs checked; drafts reviewed; messages actually sent; replies; qualified enquiries; written quotes; accepted orders; paid receipts verified; deliverables completed on time; revision rate; customer feedback. Initial baseline is **unknown** until measured. Sales and revenue remain zero/unverified unless supported by records.

## 7. Security, privacy and continuity gates
Before adding integrations or deleting content:
- Review secrets and tokens in repository history/configuration; never expose credentials in client-side HTML.
- Apply least privilege to workflows; pin third-party actions to reviewed versions/SHAs where practical.
- Validate untrusted input, escape rendered text, avoid unsafe DOM HTML insertion, and test links/forms on mobile.
- Keep a rollback path; make changes in a branch and use a pull request.
- Do not collect sensitive customer information without a clear need, notice, retention policy, and secure server-side handling.
- Browser-local tools must clearly say that their records do not automatically sync to GitHub or other devices.
- Security checklists are not proof of an actual penetration test. Record real test evidence and findings separately.

## 8. Definition of done
A change is done only when:
- the page is reachable from the canonical public entry point;
- every CTA leads to a working destination;
- mobile and keyboard navigation are checked;
- catalogue status matches the actual implementation;
- no existing unique tool or source material is lost;
- relevant automated checks pass and the result is recorded;
- external voice/video/email/payment behavior has separate reproducible evidence;
- revenue claims are backed by verified receipts, not plans or outreach activity.
