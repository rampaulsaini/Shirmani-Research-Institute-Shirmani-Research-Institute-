# Yatharth Humanity–Earth–Civilization Framework

## Purpose

This module translates the author's Yatharth vision into an explicit systems architecture for protecting human dignity, living beings, vegetation, oceans, atmosphere, forests, Earth systems, and the wider physical environment.

The repository must distinguish an author's philosophical proposal from independently established scientific or policy facts.

## Design principle

Family → community → city → region → nation → planet → wider physical universe.

Every layer is connected to the others through measurable welfare, ecological, safety, knowledge, and accountability signals.

## Core objectives

- Protect human dignity and basic needs.
- Improve access to food, clothing, shelter, healthcare, education, knowledge, connectivity, and opportunity.
- Protect forests, biodiversity, vegetation, oceans, atmosphere, soil, water, and climate-relevant systems.
- Reduce avoidable suffering while preserving individual rights, freedom, privacy, and pluralism.
- Make public decisions transparent, evidence-linked, auditable, and appealable.
- Prefer prevention and restoration over avoidable damage.
- Keep high-impact automated decisions reviewable by accountable humans.

## System layers

1. Individual and family wellbeing
2. Community services
3. Education and skills
4. Health and essential services
5. Employment, freelancing, and entrepreneurship
6. Digital commerce and creator economy
7. Environmental and biodiversity protection
8. Justice, rights, dispute resolution, and accountability
9. Research, evidence, and independent verification
10. AI/ML/NLP and Automission operations
11. Public dashboards and participatory feedback
12. Cross-border and planetary coordination

## Safety boundary

AI may monitor, classify, recommend, simulate, translate, detect anomalies, and automate routine operations. It must not become an unaccountable sovereign authority. Legal rights, high-impact sanctions, major financial actions, emergency powers, justice outcomes, and independent verification require appropriate human accountability, appeal, and audit mechanisms.

## Success measures

The system should track outcomes rather than claim them:

- access to essential services
- user/customer satisfaction
- service reliability
- educational participation and completion
- employment and income opportunities
- ecological restoration/protection indicators
- safety incidents
- complaint resolution
- model error and bias indicators
- independent audit results
- verification status

No metric should be represented as proof of universal success without evidence.
