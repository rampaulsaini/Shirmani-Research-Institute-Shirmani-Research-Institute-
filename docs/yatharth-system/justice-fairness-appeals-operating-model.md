# Yatharth Justice, Fairness & Appeals Operating Model

## Purpose

Provide a neutral, transparent governance architecture for fairness, complaints, disputes and appeals across the public platform.

## Core rule

Justice is not an AI opinion. The platform may provide information, routing, evidence organization and procedural assistance, but consequential adjudication must follow applicable law and an accountable review process.

## Public functions

- Complaint submission
- Case/status tracking
- Evidence upload and provenance
- Policy explanation
- Mediation information
- Appeal submission
- Accessibility support
- Independent-review referral
- Aggregate transparency reporting

## AI/ML/NLP functions

AI can:
- classify incoming complaints;
- detect duplicate records;
- summarize evidence;
- identify missing procedural information;
- translate submissions;
- detect potential conflicts for human review;
- monitor consistency of published rules.

AI must not:
- secretly rank people by worth;
- make irreversible punitive decisions without due process;
- suppress appeals;
- fabricate evidence;
- conceal uncertainty;
- present an automated recommendation as a final legal judgment.

## Governance separation

Keep separate:
- executive/administrative functions;
- legislative/policy functions;
- judicial/adjudicative functions;
- platform operations;
- independent audit.

The platform may model these separations digitally, but it does not replace constitutional or lawful public institutions.

## Appeal principle

Every consequential automated action should expose:
1. what happened;
2. the applicable rule;
3. material evidence used;
4. confidence/uncertainty where meaningful;
5. how to challenge it;
6. human review route;
7. expected response timeline.

## Audit

Record versioned policies, model versions, decision traces, reviewer actions and appeal outcomes subject to privacy and legal requirements.
