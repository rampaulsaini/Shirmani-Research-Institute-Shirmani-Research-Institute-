# Humanity, Earth and Living-Systems Protection Framework

## Central objective
Translate the author's Yatharth perspective into practical, testable platform mechanisms for protecting people, living systems and the physical environment.

The philosophical source remains an author source. Environmental and social outcome claims require independent evidence.

## Scope
### Human well-being
- access to knowledge and education;
- lawful work and economic opportunity;
- accessibility and inclusion;
- safety and dignity;
- support and dispute pathways.

### Living systems
- forests;
- vegetation;
- wildlife;
- biodiversity;
- freshwater;
- oceans and marine ecosystems;
- soil;
- atmosphere.

### Built environment
- responsible resource use;
- waste reduction;
- restoration;
- resilient infrastructure;
- transparent environmental reporting.

## Platform mechanisms
1. Environmental impact registry
2. Restoration project registry
3. Community reporting
4. Evidence-backed issue tracking
5. Research and education resources
6. Creator campaigns for verified causes
7. Marketplace support for sustainable products/services
8. Public outcome dashboards
9. Independent review
10. Abuse and misinformation controls

## Well-being principle
The platform should seek to reduce avoidable harm and improve access to useful resources. It must not promise that AI can eliminate all suffering or guarantee universal satisfaction.

## Family → community → nation → global systems
The architecture can model nested responsibilities:
Individual → Family → Community → City/Region → Nation → Global commons.

The model makes impacts visible across levels rather than giving any single level unchecked authority.

## Governance safeguards
Public institutions, elected officials, courts and administrators are external governance contexts, not AI-controlled entities. The platform can provide evidence, transparency tools, research and public-service interfaces, while legal authority remains with applicable institutions and laws.

## Measurement
Each major initiative should expose:
Baseline → Intervention → Evidence → Outcome → Independent Review → Revision.

This prevents aspirational language from being confused with measured real-world impact.
