# Yatharth Humanity & Earth Protection Charter

## Purpose
Define a public, evidence-aware framework for protecting human dignity, living beings, vegetation, oceans, atmosphere, forests, soil, and space environments while preserving individual agency.

## Author-source boundary
This charter records the user's stated objective and proposed framework. It does not establish metaphysical or scientific claims as independently verified facts.

## Core principle
The platform should help people move from short-term self-interest toward reflective, balanced decision-making that considers:
- individual needs and rights
- family and community
- human civilization
- animals and vegetation
- water, oceans, air, soil and forests
- planetary and wider environmental systems

## Universal basic-needs objective
The proposed public vision includes broad access to food, clothing, shelter, healthcare and education. Implementation must be treated as a policy/design proposal and evaluated for feasibility, funding, law, safety, equality and unintended effects.

## Protection workflow
Observe → document → assess evidence → model impacts → compare alternatives → disclose uncertainty → decide with accountable oversight → monitor outcomes → correct.

## No-harm engineering
AI systems must not claim that they can guarantee that nobody will ever suffer. They should instead minimize preventable harm, detect risks early, provide accessible support, and preserve appeal and human review for high-impact decisions.

## Environmental safeguards
Public products and automated agents should track, where measurable:
- resource consumption
- waste
- emissions
- biodiversity impacts
- water and land impacts
- supply-chain risks
- environmental restoration outcomes

## Governance
The platform must remain subject to applicable law, transparent rules, audit logs, privacy protections, security controls, and meaningful human appeal.

## Success measures
Separate metrics should be maintained for:
1. user safety
2. user satisfaction
3. accessibility
4. environmental outcomes
5. service reliability
6. evidence quality
7. independent review
8. automation health

No single score should be presented as proof that the whole system is successful.
