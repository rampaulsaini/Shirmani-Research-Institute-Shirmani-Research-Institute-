# Humanity, Nature and Yatharth Governance Layer

## Scope
This module translates the author's stated objective of balancing individual life, society and the natural world into a testable governance and product-design layer.

## Author-source themes
The source material emphasizes:
- a shift from a head/mind/intellect-centered viewpoint toward a heart-centered viewpoint;
- self-observation and impartial understanding;
- protection of Earth, forests, oceans, atmosphere, plants, animals and human communities;
- reducing avoidable suffering;
- access to essential capabilities such as food, housing, healthcare and education;
- balanced decision-making from family/community through larger institutions.

These are preserved as the author's framework unless independently established by evidence.

## Governance architecture
Family/community -> local institutions -> regional/national institutions -> global cooperation.

The platform itself does not replace governments, courts or constitutional institutions. It can provide research, transparency, civic information, service coordination and evidence tools.

## AI governance controls
AI agents must:
1. identify the task and authority boundary;
2. preserve source provenance;
3. distinguish facts, interpretations and proposals;
4. log consequential actions;
5. fail closed on missing authorization;
6. provide appeal/escalation paths;
7. avoid discriminatory or manipulative targeting;
8. protect personal data;
9. continuously test for failures and drift.

## Justice and fairness
Yatharth Justice is a research/product concept. It should provide transparent rules, evidence handling, dispute intake, procedural safeguards, independent review and appeal rather than an AI claiming supreme authority over people.

## Nature protection metrics
Potential public metrics include:
- forest/land protection actions;
- biodiversity indicators;
- water and ocean protection projects;
- waste and pollution reduction;
- energy/resource efficiency;
- restoration activity;
- verified community outcomes.

Metrics must be sourced and independently defined before being presented as verified results.

## Basic-needs research
A dedicated research track should evaluate models for universal access to:
- food;
- clothing;
- housing;
- healthcare;
- education;
- connectivity and essential digital services.

Each proposal requires evidence, cost model, legal constraints, implementation pathway and outcome measurement.

## Human satisfaction
The platform should measure satisfaction through voluntary feedback, resolution quality, accessibility, reliability, affordability and meaningful outcomes—not merely screen time or engagement.

## Verification boundary
AI-generated confidence, workflow success, publication, hashes or automated QC do not constitute independent human/scientific verification.
