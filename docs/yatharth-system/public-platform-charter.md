# Yatharth Public Platform Charter

## Purpose

Define the public-facing operating model for a global social, knowledge, education, creative, marketplace and research platform built around the author's Yatharth framework.

This charter is an architectural specification, not a claim that every capability is already deployed.

## Public promise

The platform should make it possible for people to:
- create accounts and profiles;
- publish and share lawful content;
- learn, teach and offer skills;
- provide and purchase services;
- sell digital products and creative work;
- participate in research and evidence review;
- use AI-assisted creation and translation;
- form communities;
- discover opportunities;
- obtain support and submit complaints or appeals.

The product goal is sustained **user value, trust, safety and satisfaction**, rather than engagement at any cost.

## Core domains

1. Social and community
2. Research and knowledge
3. Education and skills
4. AI/ML/NLP services
5. Yatharth AI Music and creative studio
6. Freelancing and employment
7. Digital Store and creator commerce
8. Yatharth economy and proposed Yatharth currency research
9. Yatharth justice, fairness and dispute-resolution research
10. Nature, Earth and biodiversity protection
11. Verification, provenance and public evidence
12. Multilingual global access
13. Accessibility and low-bandwidth access
14. Privacy, security and responsible AI

## Account model

A person may have one account with multiple roles, subject to verification and platform rules:
- individual;
- creator;
- learner;
- educator;
- researcher;
- freelancer;
- client/customer;
- seller;
- organization;
- reviewer.

Role changes must be auditable and reversible where appropriate.

## AI-native operating model

AI agents, ML and NLP may automate:
- intake and classification;
- semantic indexing;
- translation;
- recommendations;
- search;
- marketplace matching;
- moderation assistance;
- fraud/spam detection;
- customer support;
- quality checks;
- research packet generation;
- workflow orchestration;
- monitoring and recovery.

Automation must be observable, logged, testable and bounded by explicit policies.

## Human control and appeal

The platform is designed for maximum practical automation, not unaccountable machine sovereignty.

Human review and appeal remain available for high-impact decisions such as:
- account suspension or permanent removal;
- major financial disputes;
- safety escalations;
- legal/rights-sensitive complaints;
- independent scientific or factual verification;
- changes to foundational governance rules.

No AI output becomes a verified fact merely because an automated workflow succeeds.

## Nature and humanity objective

The platform may promote research, education and practical projects concerning:
- clean air, water and oceans;
- forests and biodiversity;
- climate and environmental protection;
- humane treatment of living beings;
- sustainable production and consumption;
- access to food, shelter, healthcare and education.

Specific policy proposals must remain open to evidence, feasibility analysis, lawful implementation and independent review.

## Economic access

The architecture may support free foundational resources and paid optional products/services. Any proposed currency, universal basic provision or economic model requires explicit legal, economic and operational analysis before real-world deployment.

## Trust lifecycle

**Create → Publish → Discover → Verify → Share → Transact → Deliver → Review → Resolve → Learn → Improve**

Every important state transition should have provenance and an auditable record.

## Success measures

Public dashboards should distinguish:
- users and active users;
- content and transactions;
- customer satisfaction;
- safety incidents;
- complaint-resolution time;
- accessibility;
- service reliability;
- environmental projects;
- education outcomes;
- research evidence status;
- independent verification status.

No single engagement metric should be treated as the definition of platform success.
