# Public Capability Registry

This registry defines what the public platform may expose and how each capability moves from design to production.

## Capability families

### Social
Accounts, profiles, posts, follows, comments, communities, messaging, media sharing, reporting and moderation.

### Creator
Audio, music, video, articles, books, courses, digital products, portfolios and creator analytics.

### Marketplace
Digital Store, services, freelancing, jobs, business listings, product discovery, orders, delivery and reviews.

### Education
Courses, learning paths, research library, AI/ML/NLP learning, assessments and certificates.

### Research
Yatharth source archive, claim registry, evidence registry, comparison, research packets, public reports and independent verification.

### AI
AI assistant, multilingual NLP, semantic search, recommendation, creator assistance, research assistance, service matching and support automation.

### Economy
Payments, creator earnings, marketplace accounting, pricing, refunds, tax/compliance interfaces and—only where legally implemented—any proposed Yatharth Currency functionality.

### Justice & Trust
Safety reports, complaint routing, dispute resolution information, appeals, transparency reports, provenance and audit records.

### Nature & Humanity
Environmental dashboards, conservation projects, resource information, community initiatives and wellbeing-oriented services.

## Capability lifecycle

IDEA → SPECIFIED → BUILT → TESTED → SECURITY REVIEWED → POLICY REVIEWED → PILOT → LIVE → MONITORED

A capability is not described as LIVE merely because a GitHub workflow passes.

## Public status contract

Every major capability should expose:
- status
- owner/system boundary
- data sources
- last update
- limitations
- safety controls
- user appeal/contact route
- verification state where relevant

## AI autonomy contract

Routine low-risk tasks may be automated.

High-impact actions require explicit policy gates and, where appropriate, authorized human review.

AI must not fabricate evidence, conceal uncertainty, impersonate public authority or silently alter historical records.

## User agency

Users control their own content, account settings and participation to the extent permitted by law and platform rules. Recommendation systems should provide meaningful controls rather than relying solely on engagement optimization.

## Privacy and security

Collect the minimum necessary data, protect credentials and payment information, provide deletion/export mechanisms where legally applicable, and maintain auditable access controls.
