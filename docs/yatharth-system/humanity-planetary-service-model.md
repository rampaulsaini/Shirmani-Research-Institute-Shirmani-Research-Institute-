# Humanity and Planetary Service Model

## Purpose

Translate the author's stated objective of improving protection of people, Earth and living systems into an auditable service architecture.

The framework treats this as a design objective, not as proof that the objective has already been achieved.

## Service domains

1. Food and nutrition
2. Clothing and basic material needs
3. Safe housing
4. Healthcare access
5. Education and learning
6. Clean water and sanitation
7. Energy access
8. Digital access
9. Employment and livelihoods
10. Disability and accessibility support
11. Emergency and disaster support
12. Environmental protection
13. Biodiversity and habitat protection
14. Ocean and freshwater stewardship
15. Air and pollution monitoring
16. Climate-risk information
17. Research and knowledge access
18. Community participation
19. Justice information and lawful dispute support
20. Mental and social wellbeing resources without making medical claims or replacing professionals

## Operating principle

The platform should seek to increase access to essential services through partnerships, public information, education, marketplaces, donations, lawful service providers and technology.

It must not claim that AI can guarantee that nobody will ever suffer. Instead, the measurable objective is to reduce preventable harm and improve access, response quality and user satisfaction.

## Environmental data layer

Where reliable data are available, the platform can expose:

- air quality;
- water quality;
- habitat and biodiversity indicators;
- deforestation indicators;
- waste and pollution indicators;
- disaster alerts;
- restoration projects;
- verified conservation opportunities.

Data provenance and uncertainty must be visible.

## Family-to-global model

The service model can be organized as:

Person → Family → Community → Locality → Region → Nation → Global ecosystem.

The same core principles apply at each level while respecting lawful institutions, local context, human rights and individual autonomy.

## Outcome dashboard

Track separate metrics for:

- access;
- affordability;
- service completion;
- user-reported satisfaction;
- safety incidents;
- unresolved complaints;
- environmental indicators;
- restoration activity;
- educational outcomes;
- livelihood outcomes.

No single aggregate score should conceal failures in a critical domain.
