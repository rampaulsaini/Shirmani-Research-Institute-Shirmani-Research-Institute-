# Basic Services & Human Dignity Model

## Objective

Define a public-platform research and coordination layer for essential human needs while avoiding unsupported promises of universal free services.

## Service domains

1. Food and nutrition
2. Clothing
3. Safe shelter
4. Healthcare
5. Education
6. Clean water
7. Sanitation
8. Energy access
9. Digital connectivity
10. Legal information and access to complaint mechanisms
11. Skills and employment pathways

## Platform functions

- Discover available public/community services.
- Match people to lawful programs and providers.
- Explain eligibility and required documentation.
- Support multilingual access.
- Track applications where integrations permit.
- Provide accessibility features.
- Collect service-quality feedback.
- Route unresolved issues to appropriate human institutions.
- Publish aggregated, privacy-preserving outcome metrics.

## AI responsibilities

AI may assist with:
- language translation;
- information retrieval;
- eligibility explanation;
- service matching;
- document classification;
- appointment/workflow reminders;
- accessibility adaptation;
- feedback clustering.

AI must not independently decide a person's legal entitlement, medical diagnosis, welfare eligibility, or other high-impact status unless a lawful, audited system explicitly authorizes that use with appropriate human review and appeal.

## Funding principle

Any free-service commitment must identify:
- funding source;
- provider;
- legal basis;
- geographic scope;
- eligibility;
- capacity;
- delivery mechanism;
- monitoring method.

## Public status labels

Every service must expose one of:
PLANNED, PILOT, AVAILABLE, LIMITED, INTEGRATED, PAUSED, RETIRED.

## Human dignity requirement

No user should be required to surrender unnecessary personal data merely to discover basic-service information.
