# Yatharth Public Platform Service Contract

## Public promise

The platform is intended to give people one place to create, learn, research, communicate, sell, buy, work, collaborate and participate in ecological and community activity.

The public interface must clearly distinguish:
- available now;
- in development;
- planned;
- unavailable in a user's jurisdiction;
- independently verified;
- author-defined vision.

## Account capabilities

A registered user can, when the relevant feature is deployed:
- create a profile;
- publish permitted text, audio, image and video;
- create communities;
- offer services;
- list digital products;
- buy eligible products/services;
- publish educational material;
- use supported AI tools;
- manage consent, privacy and notifications;
- view activity, earnings and transaction records where applicable;
- report content or disputes;
- request review or appeal.

## Trust and safety

Every public submission passes through configurable safety, abuse, spam, malware and policy checks. Automation must not be described as perfect. False-positive and false-negative handling, appeal paths and incident logging are required.

## Marketplace contract

Every listing must expose, where applicable:
- seller identity/profile information;
- product/service description;
- price/currency;
- delivery terms;
- refund/cancellation terms;
- rights/licensing;
- provenance or source information when relevant;
- review/report mechanisms.

## Education contract

Courses and learning materials should expose:
- creator;
- learning objectives;
- prerequisites;
- content language;
- accessibility information;
- completion status;
- assessment method;
- certificate status and issuer.

## Research contract

Research pages should distinguish:
AUTHOR_SOURCE → NORMALIZED → EVIDENCE_MAPPED → COMPARATIVE_REVIEWED → READY_FOR_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW → INDEPENDENT_REVIEWED → VERIFIED / PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED / INCONCLUSIVE / NOT_VERIFIED.

## AI transparency

For consequential AI-assisted actions, the interface should disclose:
- that AI was used;
- the relevant system or model class where practical;
- important limitations;
- whether a human reviewed the action;
- how to appeal.

## Universal-access objective

The platform may pursue broad-access or free/basic services, but the interface must never claim a service is universally free or universally available until funding, delivery, legal and operational conditions are actually satisfied.

## Satisfaction loop

Discover → Understand → Use → Receive value → Give feedback → Resolve problems → Improve.

The target is durable user value and satisfaction, not merely maximizing time spent.
