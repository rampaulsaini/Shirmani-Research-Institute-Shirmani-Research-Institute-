# Yatharth Global Platform — Public Charter

## Purpose

This charter defines a public-facing digital ecosystem intended to connect knowledge, research, education, creativity, social participation, services and economic opportunity while keeping human dignity, environmental protection, transparency and user agency as first-class requirements.

The Yatharth framework is the author's proposed philosophical model. Its metaphysical and civilizational claims are preserved as author-source claims unless independently supported.

## Public ecosystem

The platform is designed around:
- Social profiles and user-generated content
- Research and evidence
- Education and skills
- AI/ML/NLP tools
- Music, audio, video and creative work
- Freelancing, employment and services
- Digital store and creator commerce
- Community and knowledge exchange
- Nature, Earth and humanity protection
- Trust, verification, safety and appeals
- Multilingual access

## Human-benefit principle

The platform must optimize for durable user value rather than attention alone. Product metrics should include satisfaction, usefulness, safety, accessibility, successful outcomes, creator opportunity, learning outcomes, dispute resolution and environmental impact.

## Universal basic-facility aspiration

The author's vision includes broad access to essentials such as food, clothing, shelter, healthcare and education. This is a social objective, not a claim that the current platform can provide these services universally. Any real-world implementation must identify funding, legal, operational and geographic constraints.

## Nature and Earth

Nature protection is a core product objective:
- reduce waste and unnecessary resource use;
- promote restoration and conservation;
- support verified environmental projects;
- surface environmental evidence and outcomes;
- avoid engagement incentives that encourage harmful consumption.

## Scale

A long-term aspiration may target global-scale participation, including the author's stated 850-crore-user vision. Scale targets are aspirational and must not be presented as achieved results.

## Non-negotiable trust boundary

AI automation does not equal truth, justice or independent verification. High-impact decisions require auditable rules, appeal paths and appropriate human oversight.

## Lifecycle

Preserve → Normalize → Compare → Test → Automate → Audit → Human Review where required → Publish → Measure → Improve.
