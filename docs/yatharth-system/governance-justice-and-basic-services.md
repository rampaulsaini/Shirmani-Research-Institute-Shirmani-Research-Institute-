# Governance, Justice and Basic Services Architecture

## Scope

This module translates the author's vision for impartial governance into a technical and civic architecture. It does not replace constitutional government, courts, elected institutions or applicable law.

## Governance layers

### Public platform governance
Clear terms, moderation rules, transparency reports, appeals and independent review.

### Algorithmic governance
Documented model purposes, evaluation, versioning, audit trails, bias testing and rollback.

### Marketplace governance
Seller rules, buyer protection, fraud controls, refunds, disputes and transparent fees.

### Research governance
Source provenance, evidence/counter-evidence, independent review and fail-closed verification.

### Environmental governance
Evidence-based project standards and transparent outcome reporting.

## Justice workflow

Report → classify → preserve evidence → notify affected parties where appropriate → investigate → decide under published rules → explain decision → appeal → independent review where warranted → close with audit record.

AI may assist with triage and evidence organization but must not silently become the final authority over high-impact rights or legal status.

## Political/public-office boundary

The platform may provide neutral civic information, documented laws, institutional procedures and public records. It must not use personalization to manipulate political choices or infer a user's political preference.

## Basic facilities

The author's vision includes universal access to:
- food;
- clothing;
- housing;
- healthcare;
- education.

The platform can support discovery, coordination, education, fundraising and verified service delivery. Actual universal provision requires real-world institutions, funding, infrastructure and lawful partnerships.

## Supreme control principle

The phrase "supreme control" is implemented here as a bounded integrity layer: policy consistency, auditability, safety constraints and escalation—not unchecked authority by an AI, administrator, president, court or other single actor.

## Appeals

Every consequential automated or human decision should have:
- reason code;
- evidence reference;
- appeal route;
- review deadline target;
- final audit status.
