# AI Civic Governance, Justice and Accountability

## Principle

The author's Yatharth vision emphasizes impartiality. This module operationalizes that idea as procedural safeguards rather than giving an AI system unrestricted political or judicial authority.

## Governance architecture

- Constitutional/legal compliance layer
- Public-information layer
- Evidence and provenance layer
- Policy-analysis layer
- Conflict-of-interest disclosure
- Decision-record layer
- Audit layer
- Appeal and correction layer
- Independent human review
- Public transparency dashboard

## AI responsibilities

AI agents may:

- organize public records;
- summarize legislation and policy documents;
- identify conflicting evidence;
- detect missing documentation;
- translate;
- model possible consequences;
- surface relevant rules;
- monitor procedural deadlines;
- generate audit trails.

AI agents must not secretly determine a person's legal rights, punishment, eligibility, or political preference.

## Justice workflow

Intake → identity/privacy safeguards → facts → evidence → applicable rules → counter-evidence → human review → decision record → appeal → audit.

## Public transparency

For each published decision or policy analysis, where legally permissible, expose:

- source records;
- date;
- responsible institution;
- methodology;
- known limitations;
- conflicts or disclosures;
- evidence and counter-evidence;
- review status;
- appeal route.

## Independence

No single agent, administrator, political office, company, or model should have unrestricted control over the entire system.

Use separation of duties, immutable logs where appropriate, least privilege, key rotation, incident response, and independent audits.
