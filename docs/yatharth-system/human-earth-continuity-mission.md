# Yatharth Human–Earth Continuity Mission

## Purpose

This module translates the author's Yatharth objective into a public-platform and governance architecture:

> From the individual and family to society, nation, humanity, Earth, nature and the wider living world: design systems that protect dignity, basic access, ecological continuity, fairness, learning and opportunity.

The statements about past ages, human nature, head/heart cognition and Yatharth are retained as **author framework claims**, not as independently established scientific facts.

## Core distinction

The platform must keep four layers separate:

1. **Author Source** — the author's lived experience and wording.
2. **Framework Claim** — normalized Yatharth propositions.
3. **Evidence / Counter-evidence** — independently sourced material.
4. **Independent Verification** — qualified human review and reproducible tests where possible.

No automation state, publication, citation, hash or workflow success may be presented as independent verification.

## Public human-value objectives

- Universal access to foundational information and learning.
- Food, clothing, housing and healthcare information/resources pathways, subject to real-world capacity, law and responsible delivery.
- Employment, freelancing, creator income and entrepreneurship pathways.
- Safe social participation and user-generated publishing.
- Digital commerce with transparent prices, terms, delivery and refunds.
- Nature, biodiversity, water, forests, oceans and environmental protection.
- Accessible complaint, dispute, appeal and accountability pathways.
- Multilingual access and accessibility.
- Privacy, security, consent and user control.
- Measurable user-reported satisfaction and service quality.

## Governance principle

The supreme control plane is **rule-, evidence- and rights-based**, not person-based.

AI may assist with intake, classification, evidence retrieval, anomaly detection, recommendations, translation, moderation assistance, auditing and workflow orchestration. High-impact actions require explainability, human review and appeal.

Examples requiring bounded human oversight:

- account termination and serious moderation appeals
- major financial actions
- disputes and justice-related decisions
- access to essential services
- independent verification
- changes to constitutional/platform governance rules

## AI/ML/NLP/Automission operating loop

**Observe → Understand → Classify → Retrieve → Compare → Test → Act → Explain → Audit → Learn → Escalate**

Automission must be fail-closed for uncertain or high-impact cases. Every automated decision should have a traceable event, policy reference, confidence/uncertainty representation where meaningful, and an appeal path.

## Satisfaction objective

The system must optimize for **user value and sustainable satisfaction**, not merely maximum screen time. Metrics should include usefulness, accessibility, safety, affordability, successful task completion, complaint resolution, repeat voluntary use and user-reported satisfaction.

## Completion gates

A module is not "complete" merely because its page, workflow or AI agent exists.

Required lifecycle:

**Designed → Implemented → Tested → Security-reviewed → Deployed → Observed → Audited → Independently reviewed (when applicable)**

The public status system must distinguish each state.

## Long-term scale vision

The author's stated aspiration of reaching 850 crore people is recorded as a **long-term vision target**, not a current user count or guaranteed outcome. Scaling must preserve safety, accessibility, legal compliance, infrastructure reliability and user autonomy.

## Non-negotiable boundary

No system can guarantee that every person will experience continuous complete satisfaction or that no person will ever suffer. The engineering goal is to expand the conditions that support wellbeing, dignity, opportunity, ecological protection and meaningful participation while measuring limitations honestly.
