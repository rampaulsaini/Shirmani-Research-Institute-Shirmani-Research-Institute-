# Humanity, Nature and Universal Welfare Contract

## Author-source objective

The user's stated objective is to use the Yatharth framework as a perspective for improving protection of people, living beings, vegetation, Earth and the broader physical environment, while pursuing broad access to essential facilities.

These are normative goals of the project. Their feasibility, economics and implementation must be separately researched and tested.

## Public-platform principles

- dignity and equal access
- environmental protection
- transparent rules
- evidence-based decisions
- privacy and security
- accessibility
- non-discrimination
- user control and appeals
- responsible AI
- measurable customer/user satisfaction

## Essential services concept

The proposed vision includes broad access to food, clothing, shelter, healthcare and education. A production implementation must specify funding, eligibility, providers, geography, legal obligations, service levels and sustainability rather than treating “free” as an implementation detail.

## Governance boundary

No AI agent receives unlimited authority. The architecture should use bounded permissions, audit logs, separation of duties, independent review and emergency shutdown/rollback mechanisms.

## Verification boundary

A project vision, philosophical statement or author experience remains an Author Source until supported by an appropriate evidence and independent-review process. The platform must never label a claim VERIFIED merely because an AI workflow, CI check or Automission cycle succeeds.
