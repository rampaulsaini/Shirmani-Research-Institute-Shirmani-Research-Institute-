# Humanity–Earth Governance Contract

## Mission

The platform is intended to support human dignity, environmental protection, learning, creativity, opportunity and transparent cooperation. The user's philosophical framework is preserved as author source; implementation decisions remain testable engineering and governance decisions.

## Design principles

1. Human dignity before engagement metrics.
2. User value and satisfaction before maximizing time-on-platform.
3. Nature and Earth impact must be measurable where practical.
4. Equal access should be pursued without promising impossible universal free provision.
5. Safety, privacy and security are first-class system requirements.
6. Every consequential automated decision must be explainable enough for the affected user to understand the action and appeal it.
7. AI agents must not receive unlimited authority.
8. Financial, legal and justice-related capabilities require jurisdiction-aware controls.
9. Independent verification remains distinct from AI confidence, QC status and workflow success.
10. All user-generated claims must remain attributable to their source unless independently verified.

## Resource-access vision

The project may research pathways toward broad access to food, clothing, housing, healthcare and education. Any real-world entitlement, funding, distribution or public-policy implementation must be represented as a proposal and evaluated against available resources, law, evidence and operational feasibility.

## Justice architecture

The public system may provide:
- transparent rules;
- complaint intake;
- evidence organization;
- mediation support;
- appeal workflows;
- audit trails;
- independent review interfaces.

AI must not be presented as a sovereign court or as having final legal authority.

## Environmental architecture

Track measurable indicators where data exists:
- land/forest restoration;
- biodiversity;
- water quality;
- air quality;
- waste reduction;
- energy/resource use;
- project-level environmental impact.

The system should distinguish measured observations, estimates, model outputs and author/community claims.

## Governance loop

Observe -> classify -> assess evidence -> act within authority -> record -> audit -> allow appeal -> learn -> revise.

## Failure-closed rule

When evidence, authorization, identity, safety or legal requirements are insufficient, the system pauses or escalates rather than inventing certainty.
