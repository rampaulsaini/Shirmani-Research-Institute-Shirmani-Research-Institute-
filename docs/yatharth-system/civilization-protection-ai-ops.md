# Yatharth Civilization Protection — AI Operations Model

## Purpose

Define a public-platform operating model for the user's Yatharth vision while keeping implementation, evidence, law, and independent verification distinct.

The intended direction is to use AI agents, ML, NLP, and Automission for high-volume routine coordination so that human wellbeing, nature, Earth, biodiversity, education, basic needs, knowledge, creativity, and economic opportunity can be supported at scale.

## Core principle

**Observe → Understand → Protect → Serve → Verify → Improve**

The system must optimize for human and ecological value, not merely screen time.

## Public domains

- Social and creator platform
- Research and knowledge
- Education and skills
- Employment and freelancing
- Digital store and creator commerce
- Yatharth AI and AI services
- Yatharth AI Music and creative media
- Nature, Earth, biodiversity and conservation
- Justice, fairness, complaints and appeals
- Community and mutual support
- Accessibility and multilingual services
- Transparency, evidence and independent verification
- Economic participation and payments
- Public dashboards and system status

## Account and participation model

A user may create an account, maintain a profile, publish permitted content, offer services, list permitted digital products, learn, teach, collaborate, review, and participate in communities.

High-impact actions must have clear rules, audit trails, appeals, and human oversight.

## AI/ML/NLP/Automission layers

### L0 — Human and ecological principles
Policy constraints, rights, safety, privacy, accessibility, environmental objectives and non-discrimination requirements.

### L1 — Intake
Collect and normalize permitted user actions, content, marketplace events, support requests and research inputs.

### L2 — NLP understanding
Language detection, classification, entity/concept extraction, semantic search, summarization and multilingual transformation.

### L3 — Agent routing
Route tasks to specialized agents: social, research, education, commerce, creator, support, safety, verification and conservation.

### L4 — ML intelligence
Recommendation, matching, anomaly detection, abuse/spam detection, quality signals and personalization subject to privacy and fairness controls.

### L5 — Automission orchestration
Schedule, dispatch, monitor, retry and reconcile routine jobs across repositories and platform services.

### L6 — Trust and safety
Detect abuse, fraud, manipulation, unsafe content and suspicious transactions; apply proportionate automated actions and escalation.

### L7 — Human review and appeals
Escalate high-impact decisions and disputed actions to qualified human review. Preserve an appeal path.

### L8 — Independent verification
Keep author claims, normalized claims, evidence, counter-evidence and independent verification as separate states.

### L9 — Continuous audit
Measure reliability, failures, latency, user satisfaction, accessibility, environmental indicators, security and model drift.

## Basic-needs objective

The platform may support programs intended to improve access to food, clothing, shelter, healthcare and education. These are goals and service-design objectives, not claims that the platform currently provides them universally.

Any financial, medical, legal or public-service implementation must comply with applicable law and use qualified providers where required.

## Ecological protection

The public system should track and promote:

- forest and biodiversity protection
- water and ocean stewardship
- air and pollution reduction
- responsible resource use
- climate and environmental education
- restoration and conservation projects

Environmental claims must be evidence-backed and independently reviewable where presented as factual findings.

## Governance safeguards

No AI agent may be treated as an unquestionable supreme authority.

For government, judicial, financial, medical, employment, account-enforcement or other high-impact decisions, the system should provide:

1. explicit decision criteria
2. provenance and audit logs
3. human oversight where required
4. notice and explanation where appropriate
5. appeal/review mechanisms
6. fail-closed behavior when evidence or authority is insufficient

## Success metrics

Do not use a single “overall completion” percentage.

Track separate metrics for:

- public features live
- automation reliability
- agent coverage
- NLP/ML service health
- research traceability
- independent verification
- user satisfaction
- support resolution
- accessibility
- security
- ecological outcomes
- economic participation

## Scalability vision

The long-term user-consumer aspiration may be recorded as **850 crore**, but it must remain an aspirational target until independently measurable adoption data supports any reported number.

Architecture should be multilingual, mobile-first, accessible, privacy-aware, secure and horizontally scalable.

## Current implementation rule

A module is not “live” merely because a workflow, documentation page or CI check exists.

Use explicit lifecycle states:

**PLANNED → ARCHITECTED → BUILT → TESTED → DEPLOYED → LIVE → MONITORED**

For claims:

**AUTHOR_SOURCE → NORMALIZED → EVIDENCE_MAPPED → COMPARATIVE_REVIEWED → READY_FOR_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW → INDEPENDENT_REVIEWED → VERIFIED**

Never promote an unsupported author or metaphysical claim to VERIFIED solely through automation.
