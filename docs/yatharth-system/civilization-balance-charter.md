# Yatharth Civilization Balance Charter

## Purpose

This charter translates the author's Yatharth framework into a neutral, testable public-platform design objective: improve human, ecological, educational, economic, and knowledge outcomes while preserving individual freedom, dignity, safety, evidence, and appeal.

The charter is an architectural specification, not a claim that the proposed social model has already been scientifically or independently validated.

## Core orientation

The platform should support a shift from short-term, self-preservation-only optimization toward a broader balance of:

- individual wellbeing and family wellbeing
- community wellbeing and public interest
- human development and ecological protection
- material needs and education/knowledge
- head/mind/intellect and reflective/heart-oriented experience
- innovation and safety
- automation and accountable human oversight

## Non-negotiable principles

1. Human dignity and equal access.
2. Freedom of thought, expression, association, and participation subject to lawful safeguards.
3. Evidence before verification claims.
4. Transparency about what is automated and what remains human-reviewed.
5. No hidden political control or automatic political persuasion.
6. Due process, appeal, and human review for high-impact decisions.
7. Privacy and data minimization.
8. Environmental impact measurement and reduction.
9. Accessibility and multilingual participation.
10. Fail-closed operation when safety, evidence, or authorization requirements are missing.

## Basic-needs objective

The platform may support programs and partners addressing food, clothing, housing, healthcare, education, employment, and essential digital access.

These are service goals and product requirements, not a claim that the platform can itself provide every need for free to every person.

## AI/ML/NLP/Automission role

Automation should continuously:

- understand and classify content
- route users to relevant education, services, jobs, creators, and products
- detect spam, fraud, abuse, and coordinated manipulation
- monitor service quality
- measure environmental and social indicators where reliable data exists
- generate research packets and evidence maps
- detect conflicts and missing evidence
- maintain audit trails
- test workflows and recover from routine failures
- escalate high-impact or ambiguous decisions to human review

## High-impact decisions

Account suspension, financial disputes, safety-critical actions, legal/justice-related outcomes, and independent verification must have explicit authorization, auditability, appeal, and human oversight. No AI score should by itself determine a person's rights or access to essential services.

## Success measurement

Public dashboards should distinguish:

- usage and reach
- user satisfaction
- service completion
- safety incidents
- appeals and resolution
- research evidence status
- independent verification status
- accessibility
- environmental indicators
- automation reliability

No single engagement metric should be treated as the definition of human wellbeing.

## Verification boundary

The author's philosophical, experiential, metaphysical, historical, or universal claims remain author-source material until independently tested or reviewed. The platform must never convert automation success, publication, popularity, hashes, or workflow completion into scientific or independent verification.

## Long-term vision

The long-term vision is a globally accessible ecosystem in which people can learn, create, communicate, work, sell, buy, research, collaborate, and contribute to human and ecological wellbeing through a transparent AI-assisted infrastructure.
